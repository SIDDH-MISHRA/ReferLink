# ReferLink: competition analysis

**Date checked:** 24 Oct 2026. **Method:** I read each product's own website, plus press and directory listings. I did not sign up for or pay for any of them.
**Honesty notes:**
- Any number below comes from the company itself, and is marked *(self-reported)*.
- None of this is user research. What real employees think is still untested (see section 7).

---

## 1. The short answer

Most products in this space are **referral marketplaces**. They help a *job seeker* find a *stranger* inside a company and ask them for a referral. The job seeker is the customer, and usually the one who pays.

ReferLink is **not a marketplace**. It's a tool for **one employee who already gets too many referral DMs**. The employee shares one link, requests arrive in a fixed format, and the employee answers in one click. The candidate sees the answer.

**Analogy:** marketplaces are like a job board for referrals. ReferLink is like Calendly for referral requests. Calendly didn't help strangers find you; it fixed the back-and-forth with people who already wanted your time.

## 2. Who is in the market

### A. Referral marketplaces (candidate-first)

| Product | How it works | Money | Status today |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Refril** | Candidates pick a company and send a request. Verified employees (checked by LinkedIn review) accept or reject. Referrers get an AI analysis of each candidate with a "match score" and "referral risk". Status is tracked through to the offer letter. | Free: 2 requests/week. Pro: ₹499 for 30 days, 30 requests/week. Candidates pay; referrers are never paid by candidates. | Live |
| **ReferNext** (Hyderabad) | Candidates find a referrer at a company and send the job URL, resume and a note. Referrers verify with their work email. The candidate can send up to 3 nudges, then the request auto-closes. Both sides see milestones. Includes an anonymous "Talent Marketplace", points and badges. | Free: 3 requests/week. Premium: ₹399/month or ₹999 for 3 months. No refunds. | Live |
| **RefOpen** | Candidates pay to send a request to a verified employee (work-email code). The money is held, and refunded if no referral is made. Employees earn money for each completed referral. 20 countries. | The candidate pays per referral; the employee is paid. | Live |
| **Referd** | An alumni network. Every candidate is interviewed by a "Referd Expert" before reaching a referrer. Referrers post roles and track requests in a dashboard. | Not stated | Early access ("onboarding in waves"). When I checked, its job list said "No referral jobs available." |
| **Reffconnect** | Says it's a "verified job referrals & hiring marketplace". A directory describes a credit model with company tiers. | Credits (no prices published) | **Not launched.** The site shows "We're launching soon" and a waitlist. |
| **InterviewChaCha** | A referral job board since 2012. Employees post jobs and candidates apply for referrals. *(150K+ members, self-reported)* | Not found | Older; little recent coverage |
| **ReferMee, Happy2Refer, ReferMe** | Earlier Indian referral platforms (2014–2017). ReferMe charged candidates a fee based on salary. | Varied | Little recent activity found |

### B. Paid "referral" pages for individual employees

| Product | How it works |
|---|---|
| **Topmate** | Each employee has their own page (`topmate.io/name`) and sells services. The "Job Referral" category lists services from individual creators, priced from **free to ₹1,000** (for example ₹49, ₹99, ₹299, ₹450). The requests are usually priority DMs or calls. A Blind thread questions whether some sellers really work where they claim. |

**This is the closest match to ReferLink**, because it's also *one employee, one personal link*. The difference is that Topmate is a paid creator storefront, and referral is one "service" among calls and courses. It has no structured request format, no one-click decision, and no status page.

### C. Company-side referral software (sold to HR)

**RippleHire, ERIN, LinkedIn Talent Hub referrals, Zapier/Slack templates.** These help *companies* collect referrals from their *own staff*. They don't serve an employee dealing with strangers' DMs. They are a different buyer and a different problem.

### D. Free tools people already use

| Tool | What it does |
|---|---|
| **LinkedIn DMs** | The default. Unstructured, with no tracking, so candidates get ghosted. |
| **LinkedIn "Ask for a referral" button** (2018) | A job seeker asks an existing *connection*, with a pre-written message. It only works within your network, and has no inbox or decisions for the employee. |
| **A Google Form + Sheet** | Anyone can make one in 10 minutes. I couldn't find a source on how common this is, so treat it as a possible substitute, not a measured one. |

## 4. What really makes ReferLink different

1. **It's a single-player tool.** It's useful from the first employee who uses it, with no network needed. Marketplaces need candidates *and* employees at every company before they work. That's why two of the Indian ones I checked are still on a waitlist or have empty job lists.
2. **It serves the other side.** Marketplaces compete for *job seekers* (the crowded, paying side). ReferLink serves the *employee*, the scarce side that every marketplace needs and that gets ghosted-by-volume.
3. **It plugs into demand that already exists.** Candidates already DM the employee. ReferLink doesn't try to create new demand. It turns the existing flood into a queue.
4. **No money, no accounts.** It removes the "pay to get referred" model, which can feel exploitative to job seekers and may clash with some employers' rules on taking payment for referrals. (That last point is worth checking for specific companies; I didn't verify any.)
5. **Closure for the candidate without a platform.** A candidate gets a private status link without signing up anywhere. Marketplaces offer tracking too, **so tracking alone is not unique.** What's different is getting it with no account.

---

## 5. Where competitors are better (be honest about these)

1. **Discovery.** Marketplaces help candidates find referrers. ReferLink can't; it only works if the employee shares the link.
2. **Depth.** Refril and ReferNext track to interview and offer, and offer chat, nudges and AI or expert screening. ReferLink stops at "Referred / Declined".
3. **Verification.** Marketplaces prove the employee works at the company. ReferLink trusts the person sharing the link (fine for your own link, but there's no badge).
4. **Business model.** They have revenue. ReferLink has none, by design for now.
5. **Scale and polish.** Several have been live longer and are more complete.

---

## 6. Real risks

1. **The Google Form problem.** If a Form + Sheet feels "good enough", employees won't switch. ReferLink must beat it on the parts a Form can't do: checked job IDs, one-click decisions, the candidate status link, and duplicate blocking.
2. **Topmate could copy it.** It already gives every employee a personal link. Adding a "referral inbox" would be easy for them.
3. **The market may be small.** Only employees who get *many* requests feel this pain. That's likely people with a visible LinkedIn presence at well-known companies. The size of this group is unknown.
4. **Candidates may keep DMing anyway.** The link only works if the employee redirects people to it.

---

## 7. What is not known yet (to test after launch)

These are assumptions, not facts:
1. Employees who get referral DMs feel it as a real problem, not just mild noise.
2. They prefer a free, no-account tool to a Google Form.
3. Candidates will fill in a 5-step form instead of sending a quick DM.

The PRD's metrics will show 2 and 3: `board_created`, `request_submitted`, `decision_made` and the form drop-off from `request_form_step_viewed`. For 1, the cheapest test is to talk to 5 employees who post about referrals on LinkedIn.

---

## 8. How to say this in an interview

> "There are many referral marketplaces in India, like Refril, ReferNext and RefOpen. They're built for job seekers: find a stranger, often pay, ask for a referral. I looked at the other side. The employee who gets 30 DMs a week has no tool. They either ignore people or burn an hour replying. So ReferLink is a single-player tool: one link, structured requests, one-click answers, and a status page so candidates aren't ghosted. No marketplace means no cold-start problem, and it's useful from the first user. The closest substitute is honestly a Google Form, so the product has to win on what a form can't do: checked job IDs, one-click decisions and closing the loop with the candidate."
